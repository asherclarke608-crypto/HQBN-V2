Who we are

An RMT is a Navy chaplain paired with a Religious Program Specialist (RP). Together they provide religious ministry to the Marines and Sailors of the unit.

Chaplain

A Navy officer and ordained clergy member who leads worship, offers pastoral care, and advises the commander on religion, morale, and moral matters.

Religious Program Specialist

A Navy enlisted Sailor who runs the ministry's operations, supports services and events, and works alongside the chaplain, including in the field.

For everyone

We serve people of every faith and of no faith, and we support your right to practice your own beliefs.

Lay leaders

A lay leader is a service member the commanding officer appoints to lead worship for their own faith group when a chaplain cannot. This summarizes Navy policy in MILPERSMAN 1730-010. Talk to the chaplain for how it applies here.

What a lay leader is

Appointed in writing by the CO for a set period, normally 1 year, and renewable at the CO's discretion. Lay-led services are part of the Command Religious Program and under command supervision. The appointment gives no ecclesiastical status or civilian credentials.

When they are used

Mostly as a temporary accommodation in operational, isolated, or unique settings such as deployments, recruit training, and isolated duty stations, when assigned chaplains cannot provide or arrange the service. A lay leader first looks for a military chaplain or contract clergy, then for transportation to a nearby service, and only then leads a service for members of their own faith group.

Who can serve

Authorized personnel who volunteer, show high moral character, motivation, and religious interest, and bring a letter of certification from their religious organization. They represent their own religious organization and must be trained and supervised by a military chaplain. Religious Program Specialists cannot be lay leaders or lead worship.

Limits

Chaplains and contract clergy ordinarily provide rites, sacraments, and ordinances. The CO may allow a lay leader to provide specified ones to co-religionists only if their religious organization permits it, they provide its written certification, and a chaplain is consulted. Lay leaders do not have the same confidentiality protection as chaplains.

Offerings

No offerings are taken at lay-led services unless the CO authorizes it. Any money collected goes into the command religious offering fund and must be collected, accounted for, and spent under SECNAVINST 7010.6.

When it ends

When the need ends (such as the end of a deployment), when a chaplain or contract clergy can meet it, when the lay leader transfers, or at the CO's discretion. The CO can end it early if conduct hinders the program or good order and discipline.

How to become a lay leader

  1. Tell the RMT which faith group you represent and that you want to serve.
  2. Get a letter of certification from your religious organization.
  3. Meet the chaplain for an interview and training.
  4. The commanding officer appoints you in writing.
  5. Lead services under the chaplain's supervision and keep the RMT informed.

The steps are the usual process drawn from the policy above. Confirm the local procedure with the chaplain. The Marine Corps also publishes the Religious Lay Leaders Handbook (MCRP 3-30D.1). To protect privacy, we do not post the names of current lay leaders here. Ask the RMT for the current list by faith group.

What MCO 1730.6G says about the RMT

Four core competencies

Provision, Facilitation, Care, and Advisement, combined to build and sustain the spiritual fitness of authorized users. Chaplains meet the religious requirements of the Marines, Sailors, and other authorized personnel assigned to the unit.

Spiritual fitness

Commanders own spiritual fitness as part of Marine Corps Total Fitness. RMTs are trained to support leaders at every level with spiritual fitness training and education.

New check-ins

Commanders must offer every new check-in a religious needs assessment and make the Spiritual Fitness Guide available (fitness.marines.mil/Resilience/Spiritual-Fitness).

Facilitation

Chaplains and RPs coordinate faith-specific support with local religious leaders and outside religious organizations. Your personal contact information is passed on only at your request.

RP requirements

RPs assigned to operational Marine forces qualify with the table of organization and equipment weapon, and may be qualified to operate government vehicles.

Other provisions

Commanders may use CREDO (Chaplains Religious Enrichment Development Operation) to build resiliency, support RMT community service projects, and use RMTs to give basic support to refugees and displaced persons outside the command. Chaplains and RPs put command religious program duties ahead of Chaplain Corps community duties.

Summaries only. Orders are revised often, so confirm the current version with the chaplain. Read the full text of MCO 1730.6G and MILPERSMAN 1730-010, and find the rest in the Marine Corps Publications Electronic Library.

Military OneSource counseling

What you get

Short-term, confidential, non-medical counseling for individuals, couples, and families. Up to 12 sessions per issue, by phone, video, chat, or in person. Also specialty consultations (adoption, caregiver support, special needs) and the My Military OneSource app.

Helps with

Stress, burnout, anger, anxiety, grief and loss, relationships and marriage, parenting, deployments, PCS moves, money worries, and life transitions.

Who can use it

Active duty, Guard and Reserve, their immediate families, survivors, and eligible DoD expeditionary civilians. Transitioning veterans: within 1 year of service. Ages 13 to 17: a parent attends the first session.

Needs other support

Suicidal or homicidal thoughts, sexual assault, child abuse, domestic violence, substance abuse, and diagnosed conditions are not covered. Use the crisis and helpline numbers above.

How to start: call 800-342-9647 or start a secure chat, a consultant confirms eligibility, pick your preferences and a counselor, then book your first session. Counseling is not reported to command and does not affect your career. Counselors must report abuse, threats of harm to self or others, and illegal activity.

Frequently asked

Do I have to be religious to talk to a chaplain?
No. Chaplains talk with anyone about anything, whatever you believe.
Can I talk to a chaplain from a different faith?
Yes. Chaplains care for everyone, and if you need clergy from your own tradition they will help you find them.
Will my commander find out what I said?
Conversations with a chaplain acting as clergy are generally confidential, with narrow exceptions around serious safety risks.
How do I request a religious accommodation?
Start with the RMT. We can explain the process and help with the paperwork.
Can I just stop by?
Yes. We are at the Camp Courtney Chapel, Bldg 4224, 0800 to 1530. Call ahead if you want to set a time.
Can I lead a service for my own faith group?
Possibly. The commanding officer can appoint a volunteer lay leader when a chaplain cannot meet the need. See the lay leaders section and talk to the chaplain.
Does a lay leader have the same confidentiality as a chaplain?
No. Chaplain confidentiality does not extend to lay leaders, so use the chaplain for confidential conversations.
My service was not listed. What now?
Contact your unit chaplain or check OKINEWS for the latest updates.
